Agentic development yield — a heuristic model

Q ≈ k · qC · qP · qV

This isn't a law, it's a rule of thumb. The model's value isn't in the numbers, it's in the multiplicative structure: the four factors don't substitute for one another, they behave like an AND gate. If one tends to zero, the result tends to zero — however high the others are.

The four terms

k
Model capability

How strong the LLM in play is. It isn't really an independent scalar: a more capable model degrades more gracefully when the context is poor — it infers the conventions, notices the contradictions, asks for clarification. It modulates the slope of the other terms more than it scales the result.

qC
Context quality

What state the codebase is in: architectural coherence, explicit conventions, documentation, AGENTS.md. In large part it is qP settled into place — today's conventions are yesterday's instruction work.

qP
Instruction quality

How well the goal and the route to it are communicated. Not only in the first prompt: a mid-run course correction often weighs more than the opening brief.

qV
Verification quality

Tests, type system, CI, lint. This is the error-correction term, and it is largely independent of the others: it catches the mistake before it reaches main. It is also the cheapest lever, and the one a team controls most directly.

What the model doesn't say

The 0–1 scale implies linear degradation. In practice it looks more like a threshold: below a certain level of qC the agent doesn't get gradually worse — it hallucinates confidently. Which is qualitatively worse, because before you can correct the mistake you have to notice it's there.

The terms are not independent. qC is partly qP on a delay; k changes how much the others matter. Treating them as separate variables is a deliberate simplification, not a fact.

Q is not measurable as written. "Quality of the software produced" has no unit. The formula is for reasoning about where to invest, not for producing a number to put in a report.

Epistemic status: structure plausible · coefficients unmeasured · thresholds untested
